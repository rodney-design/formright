# New York — Contractor Playbook

Internal operating notes, not legal advice — see [README](./README.md).

## Where to file

**New York Department of State, Division of Corporations**, State Records
and UCC, 99 Washington Avenue, Suite 600, Albany, NY 12231. Filed under the
**Not-for-Profit Corporation Law (N-PCL)**. Submission is **by mail or
electronically** per the Department's own instructions — there isn't a
fully self-serve public e-file portal for the Certificate of Incorporation
itself the way California/Florida have; check the Department of State's
current electronic-submission process (it has been evolving) before
assuming mail is the only option for a given job.

## Setup quirks

- **N-PCL was amended effective July 1, 2014** to classify every nonprofit
  as either a **"charitable"** or **"non-charitable"** corporation (N-PCL
  § 201) — the older "Type A/B/C/D" scheme is obsolete for new filings.
  501(c)(3) organizations are **charitable** corporations. The Certificate
  of Incorporation (N-PCL § 402) must state which one applies — make sure
  the worksheet/generated Articles say "charitable corporation," not a
  stale Type A/B/C reference some older templates or secondary sources
  still use.
- If the org's activities require **consent or approval from a NY state
  official, department, board, or agency** (e.g. certain education,
  health, or housing-related nonprofits), that consent must be attached to
  the Certificate — the Certificate must otherwise affirmatively state that
  no such consent is required. Flag this to the admin team if the client's
  stated mission/programs touch a regulated area; it's easy to miss if the
  worksheet's mission field doesn't get read closely.
- Charitable corporations face **materially more oversight on dissolution**
  than a for-profit — Attorney General and/or NY Supreme Court approval is
  required before a dissolution plan distributing assets can complete
  (N-PCL Article 10/11). Not a formation-time concern, but worth knowing
  before a client asks "how hard is it to wind this down later."

## Required attachments / fields

- Entity name
- Statement of charitable vs. non-charitable status (N-PCL § 201/§ 402)
- County within New York where the office will be located
- Registered agent designation — NY allows designating the NY Secretary of
  State itself as agent for service of process (common for out-of-state
  filers), or a named individual/entity with a NY address
- Names/addresses of initial directors

## Fees & turnaround

- **$75 filing fee**, payable by cash, check, money order, or major credit
  card.
- Standard mail processing can take **weeks**; the Department of State
  offers **24-Hour**, **Same-Day**, and **2-Hour** expedited service tiers
  for time-sensitive filings — confirm current expedite fees before
  committing to a client-facing turnaround estimate.

## Common rejection reasons

- **Stale Type A/B/C/D language** instead of the current charitable/
  non-charitable statement — the single most common "looks right but is
  actually outdated" mistake given how much secondary-source content online
  still references the pre-2014 scheme.
- **Missing required consent** for a regulated activity area, or missing
  the affirmative "no consent required" statement when none applies —
  Department of State checks for one or the other, not silence.
- **Ambiguous registered agent designation** — confirm whether the client
  wants the NY Secretary of State as agent (simpler, but service of process
  then routes through the state) vs. a named agent, and that the Certificate
  reflects whichever was actually chosen.
